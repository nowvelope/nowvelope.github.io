What Nowvelope does with your data

Last updated 11 October 2026

The short version: your captures stay on your device. There is no account, no Nowvelope server, and no tracking.

What we collect

Nothing. Nowvelope has no account system and no server of its own. We don't collect, sell, or share your photos, panoramas, audio, location, or usage, and the app contains no analytics or advertising.

What the app uses on your device, and why

To build a panorama, Nowvelope uses the camera, the phone's motion sensors and, on iPhones that have one, its depth sensor. From these it measures how the camera moved while you captured and the shape of what it saw — such as floors, walls and nearby objects — so the photos line up when they're stitched together. If you choose to add location, it also records where the panorama was taken and uses the compass to record which way the panorama faces and to keep the capture lined up.

All of this, together with the photos, the finished panorama, any audio, and its name, is stored on your device in the app's own storage. It may be included in your ordinary device backups, according to your iOS and backup settings; the app doesn't separately upload or sync it. Delete a panorama and everything stored with it is deleted too.

Place naming — the one thing that uses the network

In plain terms

Nowvelope sends nothing off your device on its own, with one exception you control: place naming. When you've added location and the app names a panorama by its place, the app sends the capture's location — not your picture — to Apple's map service to look up a short place name, such as a landmark, neighborhood, or city. Naming by place is the default; you can instead name panoramas by what's in them, which looks up a place only when nothing stands out, or turn automatic naming off. Turn it off and nothing leaves on its own at all.

Nowvelope keeps only that short name with your panorama, never a street address or contact details. Names taken from what's in a panorama come from models running on your device. Capture and saving work normally with location off.

Permissions, and what happens if you decline

  • Camera — the core function, asked for when you first capture.
  • Location (optional) — asked for only after you choose to add location to your panoramas and accept an in-app explanation. Used to record where and which way a panorama was taken, to name it, and to help line up a capture. The app never asks for background or “Always” location. Decline and capture still works, without a place name or compass direction.
  • Microphone (optional) — asked for only after you choose to record ambient audio and accept an explanation. Records environmental sound only while you capture, stored on your device and played back with that panorama. No speech recognition, no transcription, no background recording. Decline and you get a normal, camera-only panorama.
  • Photos — add-only, asked for only when you save a panorama to your library. Nowvelope adds to your library; it doesn't read it.

Sharing and export

Sharing is always something you start, and it goes only where you send it. A shared or saved panorama image carries the direction it faces, when one was recorded, so apps such as Google Maps can orient it; that is a direction, not a place. It carries the location where it was taken only if you choose to include it, which is off until you turn it on. Anyone who receives an image with its location can see where it was taken.

Separately, you can export a capture's full data, after a confirmation that says what it contains. That archive can include your original photos, location, audio, and the measurements made while capturing, which can reveal a room's layout and dimensions.

If you ask us for help, the app can share a diagnostics report: technical logs, plus anything you choose to write about the problem, and never your photos, panoramas, audio, or location. If you email it to us, we use it only to troubleshoot your issue and delete it once that's resolved.

Children

Nowvelope is a general-audience camera app. It doesn't knowingly collect personal information from anyone, because it doesn't collect personal information at all.

Changes

If what Nowvelope uses, keeps, or shares changes, we update this page, and the “last updated” date above changes with it.
